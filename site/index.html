Lindsey

I've never wanted just
anyone to hire me.

I grew up here, five generations deep. On Saturdays I'd pull the open-house listings out of the paper, beg my mom to drive me around, and spend the rest of the weekend redrawing every floor plan I'd walked through. I still do that, more or less. Now there's a permit involved.

What I actually do is hard to fit on a business card. I sit with you and figure out how you live. Where the keys land. How the groceries get in from the car. Where you work on a Tuesday night, and what you want your life to look like ten years from now. Then we pin all of it to the board, find true north, and I hold us to it. Through the drawings, through the bids, through every pivot in the field. Almost no house ends up where it started. I'm the part that doesn't change.

I love old houses. I love giving a building a second life. I'll do new, but it has to belong on its street, because your house is a gift to the people who walk past it. Real materials. Rooms built for humans, not giants. I'm not the contractor, I'm just on site more than most of them.

The best thing a client ever said to me was that their house felt like a big hug. That's the job.

Have a house in mind? The older and stranger, the better.
